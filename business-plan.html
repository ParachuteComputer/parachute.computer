Parachute

A private knowledge vault your AI can read and write. Host it yourself, or let us run it.

Business Plan Version 3 July 9, 2026 Aaron Gabriel · Parachute (PBC)

Parachute is a private place to keep your notes, documents, and voice memos, connected to the AI you already use. Your AI can read and write to it, so it actually remembers your work and can act on it. You can run Parachute yourself on your own machine, or let us host it for a few dollars a month.

We're raising $150K from friends and family at a $5M cap to fund the next six months. By November we'll either be in TechStars Boulder (which adds $220K and sets up a $1M round at $20M around the new year), or we'll raise a $300–500K bridge at $10M+ to keep going. Either way the goal is the same: reach roughly 100,000 users and a self-sustaining business in 2027, on a product that costs a dollar or a few and still earns about 83% margin.

1 What we sell

Three ways to make money, all from one codebase:

  1. Hosted cloud. Parachute Cloud is the hosted version, $1 to $10 a month. Customers connect the AI they already pay for (Claude or ChatGPT), so we spend nothing on AI ourselves. And because the whole system runs serverless, a vault that's just sitting there costs us almost nothing. This is where the margin comes from.
  2. Services. We take on paid engagements running vaults for organizations. Our first is a $2k/month enterprise pilot with the Gitcoin team, who use a shared Parachute vault as their team brain. It brings in revenue today and teaches us what bigger customers need.
  3. Enterprise licensing. Parachute is open source under the AGPL, which means a company building on it either shares its own changes publicly or buys a commercial license from us. That's a 2027 revenue line. Cloud and services carry us until then.

2 Where we are today

We've shipped, and money is starting to come in.

  • We launched the self-hosted version on April 23, and about 15 committed beta users are running it today.
  • On July 7 we opened the hosted cloud with live Stripe payments, and paid users are starting to trickle in.
  • The cloud runs entirely serverless on Cloudflare (a dedicated database and object storage per vault), rebuilt this past month to replace our earlier container setup. Self-host and cloud both run from the same open-source code.

3 The market, and the long game

Parachute sits where two markets meet.

The first is the market for notes and personal-knowledge tools, already worth billions of dollars a year. Notion, Obsidian, Evernote, and Apple Notes have tens of millions of paying users between them. People clearly pay to keep their thinking somewhere good.

The second is newer and barely exists yet. As more people run their daily lives through an AI assistant, they need a private, portable place for that AI to remember things and get work done, something they actually own rather than rent from a big platform. That's the layer Parachute is built for.

We don't need a big share of either. At our current economics, around 100,000 paying users makes us self-sustaining, and a million would make us a category leader. Both are small fractions of the people already paying for a notes app. The long-term plan is to grow from a note vault into the trusted home for someone's digital life, raising what each customer is worth over time as we add our higher Surface and Agent tiers on top.

6 Unit economics and margins

The serverless rebuild is what makes such low prices work.

  • An idle vault costs us pennies a month. We only pay for storage, and a vault full of text is a few megabytes.
  • We pay nothing for AI, because customers bring their own model.
  • The only real variable costs are attachment storage and voice-transcription minutes, and both are built into the paid tiers.
  • At a $3 blended price that leaves about 83% gross margin, roughly $2.50 of margin per user.
The upshot: we can charge a dollar and still make money on it. A genuinely cheap entry point is hard for bigger, higher-cost competitors to match, and that's a real part of our moat.

8 Revenue path to break-even

  • Average revenue per user is around $3, with about $2.50 of that being margin. This is a launch-week estimate, and our first paying users are starting to give us the real number.

  • We don't need to reach break-even before the next raise. The raises are what fund the climb to 10,000, and the services work already covers part of the burn today.

Both paths are up-rounds. Even the downside case is priced, and it's still a 2x or better step up.
