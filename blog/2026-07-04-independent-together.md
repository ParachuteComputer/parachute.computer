Today is Independence Day in the country where I live, and I want to talk about a specific kind of independence — and then, because it's the part that actually matters, about its opposite.

## The dependence nobody signed

Somewhere along the way, we handed our memory over.

Your notes live in someone's box. Your conversations with AI live in someone else's box. Your voice memos, your meeting transcripts, the half-formed ideas you trusted to an app at 2am — scattered across services that rent your own mind back to you, on terms that change whenever the box-maker's incentives do. Prices go up. Features get removed. APIs close. The export button, when it exists, produces something you can't actually use anywhere else.

None of us signed up for that, exactly. It happened one convenient choice at a time. And each choice was genuinely convenient! That's what makes it hard to see: this isn't a story about villains, it's a story about architecture. **When your memory lives in a closed box, dependence isn't a bug — it's the business model.**

Independence, in this specific sense, just means: *your memory belongs to you.* Not as a slogan — as a property of the system. Your notes in a database you control, in a format you can read, with an export that always works and imports cleanly somewhere else. Whether you run it yourself or someone hosts it for you, the deal is the same: **you can always leave, so you never have to.**

That's what [Parachute](/) is. A vault for your knowledge — notes, voice, captures, the whole growing pile — that any AI can read and write, that you can host on your own machine with one command or have hosted for you, and that exports completely, always.

## But independence is a lonely story

Here's the thing, though. Nobody actually wants to be *independent* — not fully, not really. Sitting alone with your perfectly sovereign data helps no one, connects to nothing, thinks no new thoughts.

What we want is to be **connected**. To our AIs, so they can actually know us and think with us. To our tools, so our work flows instead of fragmenting. To each other — the people we build with, the teams we think with.

A parachute is a good teacher here. A parachute isn't a solo object — it's a canopy, dozens of lines, a harness, each part holding the others, none of it working alone. What makes a parachute trustworthy isn't independence from physics. It's that every connection in it is *by design* — chosen, load-tested, and holding.

So the real goal was never independence. It's **interdependence you chose** — connection with an exit, relationship without capture. And that's exactly what the closed boxes can't offer, because a connection you can't leave isn't a relationship; it's a dependency with better marketing.

This is why we build on open standards — your vault speaks [MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io), so *any* AI can connect to it, not just the one we like this year. It's why export isn't a feature we grudgingly support but the moral center of the design. And it's why the same open-source software runs whether you self-host or we host it for you: two doors, one contract, and you can walk between them with your data intact.

Independence from the boxes. Interdependence with everything — and everyone — you actually choose.

- And honestly: some parts are young. Agents are an experimental preview. Some edges are still rough. We ship carefully and fix fast, and the parts that guard your data are the parts we've hardened first.
